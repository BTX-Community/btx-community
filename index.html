    New to BTX

    6 steps

    BTX is a post-quantum fork of Bitcoin with a fixed maximum supply and its own proof-of-work (MatMul). It is run by an open community, not by a company.

    Mining

    4 steps

    Mining needs a GPU. You can mine alone against your own node and keep every block, or join a pool for steadier payouts.

    • Pick a miner →

      For example easyBTX (an app for Mac and Linux; on Windows through WSL) or matador (open source).

    • Prefer a smaller pool ↗

      When one pool finds most blocks, the network depends on it; the status page shows the current shares.

    • Use your own btx1z address →

      matador's quick start contains a sample payout address that is not yours and two pools that are offline. Put in your own address and a pool from our link list.

    • Problems with an NVIDIA card ↗

      The official CUDA troubleshooting guide; parts of it describe the CUDA path from before block 185,000.

    Running a node

    7 steps

    Every node relays blocks and keeps the network reachable. A node checks every block itself only with a GPU that passes btxd's self-test. Without one, run it as a trusted mirror that follows signed checks from at least two signers; otherwise it stops syncing. Signers have the highest requirements.

    • Easiest start at home: easynode ↗

      Installs and supervises btxd for you.

    • On a server ↗

      v0.34.16 has two Linux x86_64 archives for glibc 2.38 or newer (Ubuntu 24.04, Debian 13): a plain one, and -cuda13 for GPU nodes (sm_120: RTX 50 and RTX PRO Blackwell cards; driver not included). No official Windows or macOS archive.

    • Before you install: the checklist →

      Where to download, how to check the file, and the settings for a node with coins.

    • Start from a signed snapshot ↗

      Catches up in minutes. A sync from genesis currently stops at block 126,978 (issue #272).

    • Node stuck at a height? ↗

      The node help explains each known case and its fix.

    • Have a spare machine? →

      Seven ways to give BTX infrastructure, from a node to a fuzz box.

    • Run a signer ↗

      Signers (GPU attestors) need a GPU that passes btxd's self-test and must stay online. The official CUDA archive covers Blackwell cards (sm_120); other NVIDIA cards need a build from source and may not qualify. Follow the developers' operator runbook.

    Before you buy or receive BTX

    7 checks
    1. Make a backup before money arrives. In btxd, backupwalletbundlearchive writes one encrypted file; keep it and its passphrase offline and apart. Any backup can spend your coins: plain exports at once, the archive once opened. Restore it once to test it.
    2. Check the receiving address in full, not just its start and end. BTX addresses start with btx1z.
    3. Send a small test amount first. Wait for at least 10 confirmations before you treat a payment as final; short reorganisations of the chain happen several times a day.
    4. Before you use an exchange or an OTC seller, check that withdrawals really work. A price on a website is not a market you can sell into.
    5. Other coins also trade as BTX. A market is only for this chain if its deposit addresses start with btx1z.
    6. A "model" or "reference" price is an estimate, not an offer that anyone has to honour.
    7. Nobody legitimate asks for your seed. Messages offering help, "recovery" or guaranteed returns are scams.
